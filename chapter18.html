Chapter 18: Machine Learning (Part 2 - Models & Scikit-Learn Pipelines)

1. Decision Trees & Random Forests (`DecisionTreeClassifier`, `RandomForestClassifier`)

A Decision Tree breaks down complex choices into binary (Yes/No) questions. A Random Forest combines dozens of distinct trees into an ensemble committee that votes on final predictions to eliminate errors!

Assignment 1: Real DecisionTreeClassifier & RandomForestClassifier

2. Actual Linear Regression with `sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression`

While Classifiers predict categories (Cat vs Dog), Regression Models like sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression fit an optimal mathematical line ($y = m \cdot x + b$) to predict continuous numbers (House Prices, Temperatures, Sales)!

Assignment 2: Real Scikit-Learn LinearRegression Model

3. Master the 4-Step Scikit-Learn Pipeline Blueprint

All Scikit-Learn estimators strictly follow the exact same 4-step pipeline:

  1. Instantiate: model = ClassifierOrRegressor()
  2. Train: model.fit(X_train, y_train)
  3. Predict: predictions = model.predict(X_test)
  4. Evaluate: score = model.score(X_test, y_test)
Assignment 3: Standard 4-Step Scikit-Learn Pipeline

4. Evaluation Metrics (`precision_score`, `recall_score`, `confusion_matrix`)

Simple Accuracy can be misleading in imbalanced datasets (e.g. Fraud detection or Cancer screening). We use sklearn.metrics to compute Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and Confusion Matrices!

Assignment 4: Scikit-Learn Precision, Recall & Confusion Matrix

5. Saving & Deploying Models with `pickle` / `joblib`

Training real AI models on massive datasets can take hours or days! Once trained, we serialize the model object to disk using pickle or joblib and load it instantly inside our production Web Apps!

Assignment 5: Serializing and Deserializing Scikit-Learn Models
