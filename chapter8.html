Chapter 8: Data Visualization with Matplotlib 📊

Welcome to the Visual Realm! Raw numbers tell a story, but charts, map coordinates, and animated trajectories bring data to life. In this chapter, you will master matplotlib — Python's premier library for crafting line plots, bar charts, pie charts, scatter plots, (X, Y) world map coordinates with city pin annotations, and GIF-style trajectory step animations right inside your browser!

💡 How Matplotlib renders in the browser: We use a quick helper render_plot() to save your plot as a high-resolution image string and display it instantly below your code editor!

1. Your First Line Plot 📈

Line plots show how numbers change over time or across categories. Use plt.plot(x, y) to draw lines, and add titles with plt.title(), plt.xlabel(), and plt.ylabel().

Assignment 1: Weekly Temperature Trend

2. Bar Charts for Comparisons 📊

Bar charts compare quantities across discrete categories. Use plt.bar(x, height) for vertical bars or plt.barh(y, width) for horizontal bars!

Assignment 2: Favorite Programming Languages

3. Pie Charts for Proportions 🥧

Pie charts display parts of a whole percentage. Use plt.pie(sizes, labels=labels, autopct='%1.1f%%') to add percentage labels and explode slice offsets!

Assignment 3: Adventurer's Daily Time Breakdown

4. (X, Y) World Map Coordinates & Pin Annotations 🌍📍

Geographic data is mapped using 2D $(X, Y)$ spatial coordinates, where $X$ represents Longitude (-180° to +180°) and $Y$ represents Latitude (-90° to +90°). Use plt.annotate() to pinpoint cities with text labels and arrows, and plt.plot() to draw flight connections!

Assignment 4: World Map City Pins & Flight Routes

5. GIF-Style Animated Trajectory & Radar Path 🚀🎬

You can visualize step-by-step movement (like radar tracking or satellite trajectories) in a "GIF-style" progression by plotting historic positions with fading opacity (alpha trail) and highlighting the active lead marker!
